Calls to Action for a Rank and Rent Site

Which contact options this site should offer, how each one gets counted as a lead, and how that ties to billing the contractor.

What the site has now

  • Phone call. Tap-to-call number in the header, footer, About page and the fixed mobile bar.
  • Callback form. "Request an appointment" on the homepage and "Free elevation survey" on service and area pages. It asks for name, phone, address or ZIP and the main symptom (homepage only).
  • Not yet: text messaging, and the form doesn't send anywhere yet. It only shows a thank-you message.

How tracking works

Use a tracking number you own and point it to the contractor. When a tenant leaves, you point it to the next one and the number on the site never changes. Tracking tools record each call, its length and the caller's number, which becomes the lead log you show the tenant.

Pay-per-call networks typically charge only for calls that pass a minimum length, usually 60–90 seconds. Use the same rule with your tenant so short calls and wrong numbers don't count.

Billing models

ModelHow it worksBest when
Pay per leadContractor pays for each qualified call or form. Lower risk for them, but you must track every lead and agree on what qualifies.The first months, while you're proving the site works.
Flat monthly feeOne contractor pays a fixed amount for all leads. Predictable and simple once there's trust.Once you have a few months of lead history to set the price.
Revenue shareA percentage of each job closed from the site.Rarely. You depend on the contractor to report jobs honestly.

A common path is to start with a free trial or pay-per-lead, then move to a monthly fee once the contractor has seen the leads come in. A simple way to set the fee is to multiply the average leads per month by what a lead is worth to the contractor, and charge a share of that.

Form and text consent

If the contractor will call or text form leads with automated tools, the form needs a consent line that names the business, with a timestamped record. The FCC's 2023 "one-to-one" consent rule was struck down in January 2025 and never took effect, but prior express written consent for automated marketing calls and texts still applies. A 2026 Fifth Circuit decision (which covers Texas) questioned that requirement, so check with a lawyer before relying on it.

What I think

Make calls the main action, with the form as backup. Foundation repair is a large, worrying job, so people want to talk to someone. Calls are also the easiest leads to prove.

Count both calls and forms as leads, but keep the form short. Name, phone and ZIP. Each extra field loses submissions but improves lead quality, so the symptom dropdown is optional.

Add "Text us" to the mobile bar. It's cheap to add if the tracking number supports texting, and photos of cracks help the contractor quote.

Start per-lead, move to a monthly fee. Per-lead gets a contractor to say yes. Once you have 2–3 months of call logs, offer a flat fee for steady income.

Changes I'd make to the site

  1. Replace the placeholder number with a tracking number you own.
  2. Connect the forms so each lead is emailed to you and the contractor and saved in a log.
  3. Add a consent line under the form submit button.
  4. Add "Text us" to the mobile bar next to Call Now and Free Estimate.
